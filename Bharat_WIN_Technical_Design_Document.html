SECTION 1 — Executive Summary

1.1 The Operational Challenge

River basin flooding, coastal storm surges, and monsoon-triggered flash inundations constitute the deadliest and economically most destructive natural disasters worldwide. Traditional disaster management pipelines rely almost exclusively on optical satellite constellations (such as Sentinel-2 MSI and Landsat-8/9 OLI) for extent mapping. However, optical sensors are rendered 100% blind precisely during major flood crises due to dense monsoonal cloud decks, heavy convective rainfall, atmospheric particulates, and nighttime orbits. Furthermore, existing Earth observation services only provide flat, 2D binary masks indicating the presence or absence of water; they fail entirely to provide continuous water depth (h) or flow velocity vectors. As a result, incident command centers remain blind to which road arteries are safe for high-clearance rescue vehicles versus which are lethal submerged drop-offs. Manual GIS workflows require 24 to 48 hours of calibration, orthorectification, and thresholding—surpassing the life-critical 72-hour search-and-rescue window.

1.2 The Technological Solution

Flood Intelligence 3D addresses this paradigm through a cloud-native, deep-learning, and physics-constrained Earth observation engine. The system operates on active Copernicus Sentinel-1 C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) sensors emitting microwaves at 5.405 GHz, which penetrate cloud cover, heavy monsoon precipitation, and dense smoke 24 hours a day, independent of sunlight. Rather than treating flood depth estimation as an unconstrained computer vision regression task, the platform introduces a dual-stage architecture:

  • Stage 1 (Extent Segmentation): A high-resolution U-Net++ with an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 encoder and dense nested skip pathways that segments water-land boundaries from dual-polarization (VV + VH) backscatter decibel tensors.
  • Stage 2 (Hydrodynamic PINN): A Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) that couples the predicted inundation probability map with 30-meter Copernicus DEM topography and ECMWF ERA5 precipitation forcing. The network directly minimizes the residual of the 2D Shallow Water Equations and Manning's hydraulic friction laws, reconstructing a physically plausible, continuous water depth field h(x, y) in meters without requiring expensive physical in-situ depth gauges.

The entire engine is exposed through an interactive, WebGL-powered 3D dashboard built with Next.js 14 and React Three Fiber, featuring dynamic DEM displacement and interactive cross-sectional bathymetric depth slicing.

1.3 Strategic Impact & Real-World Utility

The platform compresses the end-to-end disaster intelligence lifecycle from 48 hours to approximately 2.5 seconds upon satellite raster ingest. Rescue boats, amphibious vehicles, and disaster response forces (NDRF/SDRF) receive sub-meter bathymetric profiles that prevent catastrophic grounding and vehicle submersions. Key critical infrastructure facilities—including hospitals, drinking water stations, and electrical substations—are automatically cross-referenced against water depth contours, triggering autonomous emergency warning cards. Deployed globally on Vercel's edge network, the platform is 100% open-source and democratizes geospatial intelligence by eliminating the need for expensive proprietary GIS software licenses (ArcGIS/ERDAS) or high-end local workstations.

SECTION 2 — Problem Formulation

2.1 Cloud-Cover Blindness of Optical Satellites

Optical Earth observation instruments capture reflected solar radiation across visible, near-infrared (NIR), and shortwave-infrared (SWIR) wavelengths (λ ≈ 0.4 µm – 2.2 µm). During severe meteorological disturbances—such as monsoon depressions, cloudbursts, and tropical cyclones—deep convective storm clouds exhibit near-100% reflectance across these bands, creating total optical occlusion. Furthermore, flood peaks frequently occur during nocturnal hours when optical sensors are inoperable. For example, during the 2024 Assam Brahmaputra floods and the 2022 Pakistan super-floods, optical satellites remained cloud-obscured for 11 consecutive days during the peak inundation crest, paralyzing rescue logistics.

2.2 Static Boundaries Without Depth Information

Conventional operational flood mapping algorithms—such as the Normalized Difference Water Index (NDNDWI) or basic histogram thresholding—output an unweighted binary matrix:

M(x, y) ∈ {0, 1}

While a binary mask reveals where standing water exists, it treats a 5-centimeter street puddle on an asphalt road identically to a lethal 4-meter surge submerging an underpass. Both pixels are tagged as "water." For military search-and-rescue teams, disaster management authorities, and civil defense units, a flat binary map is dangerously insufficient for navigation.

2.3 Rescue Blindspots Due to Missing Bathymetry

Underwater terrain morphology (bathymetry) and overland hydraulic heads determine how water surges through landscapes. Without elevation-coupled depth modeling, relief convoys lack knowledge of:

  • Submerged bridge decks experiencing severe hydraulic scour or displacement.
  • Low-lying drainage culverts generating fatal vortex suction currents.
  • Sudden elevation drop-offs that overturn rescue dinghies and heavy rescue trucks.

Existing hydrodynamic modeling suites (such as HEC-RAS or TELEMAC-2D) require hours of numerical grid mesh generation, boundary condition calibration, and supercomputing clusters, making them impossible to run in real-time on fresh satellite passes during active crisis operations.

2.4 Delayed Manual GIS Processing (24–48 Hours)

Standard disaster response workflows follow a cumbersome manual chain:

  1. Incident commanders request satellite acquisition (International Disaster Charter activation).
  2. Raw Level-0/Level-1 SAR products arrive as 1 GB to 4 GB archives.
  3. GIS specialists manually apply precise orbit ephemerides, radiometric calibration (β° → σ°), speckle filters, and range-Doppler terrain corrections in desktop tools like ESA SNAP.
  4. Analysts perform manual binarization and manually compile presentation maps.

By the time static maps are emailed to field squads 24 to 48 hours later, flood crests have already migrated downstream, rendering the intelligence obsolete.

4.1 End-to-End Pipeline Architecture

Figure 4.1: End-to-End Ingestion, Preprocessing, Neural Inference & Geospatial Serialization Pipeline

4.2 Step-by-Step Pipeline Execution

  1. User Upload: User drags a Sentinel-1 Level-1 Ground Range Detected (GRD) GeoTIFF into the web interface. The client validates the file format, extracts bounding-box coordinates, and packages the binary stream into a multipart/form-data POST request to /api/predict.
  2. Preprocessing & Tensor Transformation: The server receives the raw payload. Rasterio unpacks Band 1 (VV) and Band 2 (VH). Radiometric calibration converts digital numbers to backscatter coefficients:
    σ° [dB] = 10 · log₁₀(DN²) − Kcal
    A 7 × 7 Refined Lee speckle filter suppresses granular radar noise while preserving edge boundaries. The channels are clipped and normalized:
    xVV ∈ [−28.4 dB, +2.1 dB] → [0.0, 1.0],     xVH ∈ [−34.8 dB, −6.5 dB] → [0.0, 1.0]
    The resulting array is transferred to GPU memory as a shape [1, 2, 512, 512] PyTorch tensor (latency: 50 ms).
  3. U-Net++ Extent Segmentation: The normalized tensor feeds into the ResNet-34 encoder. Features pass through four downsampling blocks, a 1024-channel bottleneck, and dense nested skip decoder pathways. A final 1 × 1 convolution with a Sigmoid activation yields the continuous flood probability tensor:
    P̂flood ∈ [0.0, 1.0]1 × 1 × 512 × 512    (latency: 120 ms)
  4. Bayesian Cutoff Thresholding: Applying a calibrated decision threshold (τ = 0.50) transforms the continuous probability map into an unambiguous binary water-land mask Mflood ∈ {0, 1}.
  5. PINN Hydrodynamic Depth Estimation: The binary probability map is concatenated with the aligned Copernicus DEM elevation raster and ECMWF ERA5 24h precipitation total into a 3-channel tensor [1, 3, 512, 512]. The PINN solves the 2D Shallow Water Equations, outputting continuous flood depth h(x, y) in meters (latency: 45 ms).
  6. Risk Classification & Infrastructure Cross-Referencing: The depth raster is evaluated against building and roadway footprints:
    • Low: h < 0.5 m (navigable by emergency trucks)
    • Moderate: 0.5 m ≤ h < 1.5 m (impassable to road vehicles; boats required)
    • High: 1.5 m ≤ h < 3.0 m (structural flooding of ground floors)
    • Critical: h ≥ 3.0 m (roof-level inundation; bridge collapse risks)
  7. Output Serialization: The engine renders a color-mapped RGBA PNG encoded as a base64 string, a 10-page institutional situation report compiled via jsPDF, a spec-compliant 24-bit uncompressed RGB TIFF 6.0 file, and a JSON summary payload returned to the client (total elapsed time: ~2.5 seconds).

SECTION 5 — AI Model Architecture & Physics Formulations

5.1 Stage 1: U-Net++ Flood Segmentation

Standard U-Net architectures utilize plain skip connections that directly concatenate early encoder feature maps with late decoder layers. This introduces a significant semantic gap because low-level encoder features contain raw, unrefined spatial textures, whereas high-level decoder features represent abstracted semantic concepts. U-Net++ bridges this gap through a dense hierarchy of nested skip pathways, enabling gradual semantic alignment: 

Encoder X0,0 ────────> Node X0,1 ────────> Node X0,2 ────────> Node X0,3 ────────> Output X0,4
    │                 ▲   │              ▲   │              ▲   │              ▲
    ▼ (Downsample)   ╱    ▼ (Downsample)╱    ▼ (Downsample)╱    ▼ (Downsample)╱
Encoder X1,0 ────────> Node X1,1 ────────> Node X1,2 ────────> Node X1,3
    │                 ▲   │              ▲   │
    ▼ (Downsample)   ╱    ▼ (Downsample)╱    ▼
Encoder X2,0 ────────> Node X2,1 ────────> Node X2,2
    │                 ▲   │
    ▼ (Downsample)   ╱    ▼
Encoder X3,0 ────────> Node X3,1
    │                 ▲
    ▼ (Downsample)   ╱
Bottleneck X4,0 ─────┘

Node Update Formulation:

Let xi,j denote the output of node Xi,j, where i indexes the downsampling layer along the encoder and j indexes the skip pathway:

xi,j = ℋ(xi−1,0)   [for j = 0]   |   ℋ([ [xi,k]k=0...j−1, 𝒰(xi+1,j−1) ])   [for j > 0]

Where ℋ(·) is a convolution block consisting of two 3 × 3 convolutions followed by BatchNorm and ReLU; 𝒰(·) is an upsampling layer; and [·] denotes channel concatenation.

Hyperparameters & Loss Objective:

The network is optimized using a compound loss function balancing pixel-level cross-entropy with region-level spatial overlap:

ℒsegmentation = 0.5 · ℒFocal + 1.0 · ℒDice
ℒFocal = −αt (1 − pt)γ log(pt),    with γ = 2.0, α = 0.25
ℒDice = 1 − [ 2 ∑ yi ŷi + ε ] / [ ∑ yi + ∑ ŷi + ε ],    with ε = 1.0

5.2 Stage 2: Hydrodynamic Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN)

Estimating continuous water depth h(x, y) purely from satellite radar backscatter is mathematically ill-posed, as radar microwaves do not penetrate beneath the water surface. Our PINN resolves this by treating the depth field as a physical solution to the 2D Shallow Water Equations (SWE) governed by gravity, topographic slope, and surface roughness friction.

Governing Hydrodynamic Equations:

The 2D depth-averaged conservation of mass (continuity equation) with atmospheric precipitation inflow is:

∂h/∂t + ∇ · (h v) = R(t)   ⟹   ∂h/∂t + ∂(uh)/∂x + ∂(vh)/∂y = R(t)

Where h(x, y, t) is water depth (m); v = (u, v) is depth-averaged flow velocity (m/s); R(t) is precipitation rate forcing (m/s) derived from ECMWF ERA5; and η(x, y) = zbed(x, y) + h(x, y) is the free water surface elevation (m), with zbed representing the Copernicus DEM terrain elevation.

The steady-state flow velocity magnitude is governed by Manning's Hydraulic Friction Equation:

||v|| = (1 / n) · Rh2/3 · S₀1/2 ≈ (1 / n) · h2/3 · ||∇η||1/2

Where n is Manning’s roughness coefficient (n = 0.035 s/m1/3 for natural floodplains) and S₀ is the topographic energy slope.

PINN Multi-Term Physics Loss Function:

ℒPINN = 3.0 · ℒconsistency + 1.0 · ℒsmoothness + 0.5 · ℒcontinuity + 2.0 · ℒnonneg + 0.3 · ℒweak_supervision
  • Extent Consistency Loss (ℒconsistency): Enforces depth to be zero in dry pixels (M=0) and strictly positive in inundated pixels (M=1).
  • Surface Smoothness Loss (ℒsmoothness): Minimizes total variation across the water surface elevation η = zbed + ĥ, preventing unphysical spiky water levels.
  • Continuity PDE Residual Loss (ℒcontinuity): Directly computes the physical PDE residual using finite-difference gradient operators on the tensor grid:
    ℒcontinuity = || ∇ · ( ĥ · (1/n) ĥ2/3 ||∇η||1/2 [∇η / ||∇η||] ) − R(t) ||₂²
  • Non-Negativity Penalty (ℒnonneg): Penalizes any negative depth values: ∑ (min(0, ĥi,j))².
  • Weak Supervision Loss (ℒweak_supervision): Aligns predictions with benchmark hydraulic gauge records from global historical events (Bolivia 2018 event mean depth error: 0.14 m).

7.2 Brute-Force Countermeasures & Session Protection

  • Attempt Limiter: If an incorrect 6-digit OTP is submitted 5 consecutive times, the session is locked and the pending code is invalidated.
  • Entropy Distribution: 6-digit tokens are generated using cryptographically secure random integers (N ∈ [100000, 999999]), guaranteeing non-predictability.
  • Password Salting: Passwords are never stored in plaintext; they are hashed via SHA-256 with application-level salting.
  • Guest Gating: All dashboard and API routes are secured behind session-validation checks, redirecting unauthenticated visitors to the login gate.

Appendix D: Engineering Glossary

  • SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar): Active microwave imaging system synthesizing a large antenna aperture through flight path movement, delivering high spatial resolution day and night through dense clouds and precipitation.
  • σ° (Sigma Naught): Normalized radar backscattering coefficient expressed in decibels (dB), representing radar cross-section per unit surface area.
  • DEM (Digital Elevation Model): Quantitative raster representation of bare-earth topographic surface heights.
  • PINN (Physics-Informed Neural Network): Deep neural network architecture that embeds physical partial differential equations directly into the training objective function, enforcing physical law compliance.
  • IoU (Intersection-over-Union): Jaccard index measuring the overlap ratio between predicted segmentation pixels and ground-truth validation masks.
  • Manning's Equation: Empirical hydraulic formula relating the flow velocity of water in an open channel to hydraulic radius, channel slope, and surface roughness factor n.
